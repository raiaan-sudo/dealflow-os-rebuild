# Psychology Rules

DealFlow outbound should feel like a competent local operator asking for a small
decision, not a generic sales blast. The goal is replies, useful conversations,
and appointments from people who can be helped without deception or pressure.

## Core Principles

| Principle | Use It Like This | Failure Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pattern interrupt | Acknowledge the interruption: "I know this is out of the blue, so I will be brief." | Overly clever openers that feel scripted. |
| Specificity | Reference market, timeline, property type, search criteria, or selling situation. | Vague "real estate opportunities" language. |
| Low-friction ask | Ask for a yes/no, one-word reply, or 10-minute fit check. | Asking for a full consultation before trust exists. |
| Curiosity gap | Offer one concrete unknown: "worth checking whether your budget still works in [area]." | Clickbait or fake insider claims. |
| Problem before solution | Name the buying/selling friction before the offer. | Pitching service before relevance is clear. |
| Loss aversion | Frame avoidable waste: overpaying, stale listing, bad timing, missed prep. | Fear-based pressure or fake deadlines. |
| Status quo disruption | Show why doing nothing may keep them guessing. | Insulting their current plan. |
| Trust building | Be clear about identity, reason, limits, and opt-out. | Hiding the sender or pretending familiarity. |
| Proof | Use real, approved proof only: local process, recent comparable demand, brokerage capability. | Fabricated results or guaranteed outcomes. |
| Authority without arrogance | "I can help you compare options" beats "I am the expert you need." | Talking down to the lead. |
| Urgency without deception | Tie timing to a real decision window or list build. | False scarcity or "act now" pressure. |
| Objection preemption | Address "not ready" and "send info" with easy next steps. | Arguing with objections. |
| Conversational tone | Short sentences, plain words, one idea per message. | Corporate, hype, or AI-sounding copy. |
| Micro-commitments | Move from reply to criteria to call to appointment. | Jumping straight to "book now." |

## What Drives Replies

- The message is obviously for a real estate situation the lead recognizes.
- The ask is easy to answer.
- The sender identity is clear.
- The offer reduces work: shortlist, pricing check, timeline plan, prep list.
- The copy gives the lead permission to say no.
- The message sounds like a person, not an automation.

## What Kills Replies

- Multiple CTAs in one message.
- Big claims with no reason to believe.
- "Hope you are well" filler.
- Long paragraphs before the point.
- Over-personalization from data the lead did not knowingly provide.
- Salesy adjectives: exclusive, insane, unbeatable, guaranteed, hottest.
- Asking "when can we hop on a call?" before value is established.

## What Drives Appointments

- A clear reason the appointment is worth the time.
- A specific deliverable: price range, buyer demand check, shortlist, timeline,
  offer strategy, listing recovery plan.
- A low-risk time ask: 10 to 15 minutes.
- A natural bridge from the lead's reply.
- Confidence without pressure: "If it is not useful, no problem."

## What Creates Spam Complaints

- Missing opt-out language where required.
- The lead cannot tell who is contacting them.
- The message implies surveillance: "I saw you have equity and may move."
- The message repeats after a negative or angry reply.
- Too many follow-ups too quickly.
- Hype, emojis, all caps, fake urgency, or "last chance" language.

## What Creates Distrust

- "I have a buyer for your home" without a real approved buyer match.
- "Private listings" framed as secret access.
- Misleading referral language.
- Mentioning sensitive inferred data such as divorce, credit, income, age,
  family status, illness, debt, or distress.
- Dodging "how did you get my number?"

## Why Generic GPT Copy Fails

Generic GPT copy often optimizes for polished language instead of reply behavior.
It tends to over-explain, over-promise, stack benefits, use fake enthusiasm, and
miss compliance context. DealFlow copy should optimize for:

- one market-specific problem;
- one useful offer;
- one clear CTA;
- one safe compliance posture;
- one natural next step.
